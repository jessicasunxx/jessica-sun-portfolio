Generative Text on ESP32 TTGO T-Display

For this assignment, I created a small generative art piece that runs entirely on an ESP32 TTGO T-Display. The screen continually re-composes a short text in response to time and randomness, so the piece never loops in exactly the same way twice during the installation.

Generative System

The sketch breaks the text into smaller units (words, phrases, or characters) and uses randomness and timing to decide which fragments appear, where on the screen they land, and how they transition in and out. Instead of a fixed animation, parameters like delay, position, and color vary over time, so the display is always in a different state.

To keep the installation readable in passing, I balanced unpredictability with structure: core phrases return regularly while smaller variations (ordering, spacing, and subtle movement) keep the piece from feeling like a looped GIF.

Installation in Milstein

During class, the ESP32 ran from a battery and was hung in a small paper envelope in the fifth floor Milstein hallway near the elevators. The envelope framed the tiny screen and hid most of the wiring so that the glowing text felt like a small window rather than a piece of lab equipment.

Reflection

This project pushed me to think about code as a medium for composing time and text, not just for making something "work." Instead of debugging toward a single correct output, I was tuning a space of possible behaviors so that the piece would feel alive but not chaotic when people walked past it.

Working within the constraints of the small screen, limited color palette, and battery life made me more intentional about each visual decision. It also made me notice practical details—like update speed, brightness, and text size—that quietly shape how readable and inviting the piece feels in public.
